Inductance from a partial-inductance (Neumann) calculation of each wire model, and the series capacitance that resonates it. As references: the RGC proton loop model needs about 12 pF at 212.6 MHz, and the RGC cold deuteron board spanned 40–82 pF (36 pF fixed plus two 2–23 pF varactors).

What the model does and doesn't include

  • Quasi-static Biot–Savart fields of thin-wire models, with the currents imposed. Birdcages sit in their ideal uniform mode; Alderman–Grant strips share current evenly across 5 filaments. No eddy currents in the bath, microwave horn or frame.
  • Preset coils are computed offline at full resolution. Coils you add are computed in the browser on coarser grids (0.25 mm map pixels, 11 slices along z); raster fractions agree with full resolution to within 0.005.
  • The RGC coil is a 3-turn flat spiral (r 7–13 mm) in a plane 16 mm above the cell axis. Those dimensions were fitted to the two fractions quoted for Fig. 8 (0.56 centred, 0.43 at 1 mm away); replace them with the as-built values. For the 20 mm cell the plane moves out by the extra 2.5 mm of cell radius.
  • η is the transverse filling factor ∫cell BT² dV / (μ₀L); signal size scales as η·Q.
  • Birdcage L is the energy-equivalent value with the largest rung current set to 1 A. Its capacitance column is the value needed in each rung of a low-pass birdcage, from equating stored magnetic and electric energy in the uniform mode (Crung = (N/2)/(ω²L)). The Q-meter then sees it through a matching network.
  • Tuning flags at 212.6 MHz: 6 pF or more is in the range of the RGC proton loop; 3–6 pF is tight; under 3 pF is comparable to stray and cable capacitance, so split the coil with series capacitors or use fewer turns. A wire run longer than λ/10 carries visibly non-uniform current.
  • L does not include the leads to the tank circuit. Leads add inductance, so treat these as lower bounds.
  • Deuteron signals use the absorption-only Dulya lineshape from DeuteronPeakFit with A = 0.0782, ωQ = 27.2 kHz, ωL = 32.698 MHz, from a fit to its RGC example event. The two zones share these parameters and differ only in r. The peak fit here frees G and r; freeing A, ωQ, ωL, η and ξ as well changes P by less than 10⁻⁴ in checks against the full fit.
  • The area method assumes a calibration constant taken on uniformly polarized material. RGC deuteron calibrations came from peak fits on golden events, so if a golden event was itself non-uniform, its peak-fit bias passes into every area-based value that uses that constant.
